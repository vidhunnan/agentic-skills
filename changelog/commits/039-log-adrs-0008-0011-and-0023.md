### `docs/decisions/0023-site-content-is-split-by-kind-data-vs-prose.md` (new, 113 lines)

- Supersedes [0016](../../docs/decisions/0016-site-content-is-data-driven-from-one-source.md)
  — the one supersession here that is **not a reversal**. 0016 was **false as written**:
  it claimed *"a single content module; the components render it rather than embedding
  copy,"* while roughly fifty user-facing strings — every headline and every intro
  paragraph — had always been literals in components.
- The file records the defect that claim produced, quoting `skills.ts`: the site said
  *"six skills"* in six places long after there were eleven, because 0016 made the
  skill **data** single-source and left the copy **about** it as string literals.
- 0023 states the split that was actually in force: everything repeated or counted
  lives in `skills.ts`, section prose lives in the component that renders it, and
  every count is derived (`TOTAL_SKILLS`, `spellCount()`, `TOTAL_SKILLS_WORD`) so no
  sentence states a number a human typed. `Loop.tsx`'s hardcoded numbers are the named
  exception, declared where they live.
- Its Consequences name the cost honestly: **the boundary is a convention, not a
  mechanism** — `next build` and `tsc --noEmit`, the repo's only two quality gates,
  catch neither a literal count nor a prose string that contradicts the data.
- **Rationale:** both alternatives read `*(reason not stated)*` — why the prose was
  left in the components, and why 0016 was superseded rather than honoured by moving
  the strings.
